About

Rev A

I am a student at Texas A&M who learned engineering by building things with a team under a deadline. In FRC I worked on the build side for four years, and I led it as Build Captain for three of those years. We made it to states twice. That chapter is behind me now, but it is where I learned most of what I use.

I like projects where the mechanical design, the electronics, and the control all have to work together. Right now I am working on the drone, a rocket with TAMU SEDS, and mechanical design on the POBS team.

Vector-Thrust Tilting-Propeller Drone

Most multirotors steer by leaning. This one steers by pointing its thrust. Tilting the propellers lets the same motors push forward, sideways, or straight up, so the airframe can stay level while it moves.

It is my current project, and it pulls together mechanical design, electronics, and control in one machine. Click and hold, then drag left and right to tilt the propellers and watch the thrust split into lift and sideways push. Drag up and down to climb or descend.

Rocket

A rocket in progress with TAMU SEDS, the student space group on campus. Rocketry rewards careful engineering because every gram and every joint matters. Click and hold, then drag up and down to throttle the engine and left and right to tilt the rocket, or use the slider for throttle.

Thrust, weight, and drag all pull on a rocket at once, so a small change in one part shows up everywhere else. That is what makes it fun to design. The readouts come from a simple model, so push the throttle past about half to lift off.

DWG-003POBS mechanical team

Testbed Pipe Clamp

I designed the clamp that holds the main electronics PVC pipe to the body of our testbed. A good pipe clamp holds firmly, stays out of the way, and is easy to adjust. Click and hold, then drag up to loosen the bolts and down to tighten them. Drag left or right to push sideways on the pipe and see whether the clamp holds it. The slider sets the bolt gap.

It looks simple, but a pipe clamp has to resist vibration, hold a round pipe without crushing it, and stay easy to service. Loosen it and watch the pipe start to rattle. The details are where a part like this earns its place.

DWG-004Build

Hoverboard

A hoverboard that floats on eddy currents. Moving magnets induce currents in a conductive surface, those currents make magnetic fields of their own, and the two push against each other. Faster magnets mean more lift, so the board rises. Click and hold, then drag up to spin the magnets faster. Once the board is floating, drag left or right to tilt it and slide along the plate. The slider sets the magnet speed.

Eddy-current levitation only works while the magnets keep moving relative to the surface. Stop them and the lift fades, so the board settles back down.

DWG-005Build

Romi Robot

A Romi robot built from the ground up. Romi is Pololu's small round robot with two drive wheels, a good platform for drivetrain and control ideas. Left alone it roams the arena. Click and hold to make it chase your cursor, and use the slider to change its top speed.

Differential drive turns by running one wheel faster than the other. The same idea sits under a lot of mobile robots, from floor cleaners to warehouse carts.

Story

How I got here

Started in FRC. Still building.

It started with FRC. I spent four years on FRC Team 6901 (I am not really involved anymore), learning how a design turns into a real machine under a deadline, and I spent three of them as Build Captain, in charge of the build side of the robot. We made it to states twice.

When I got to Texas A&M I wanted the same mix of hard problems and teammates who care. So I joined TAMU SEDS to work on a rocket, the POBS mechanical team, where I designed the clamp that holds the main electronics pipe to the testbed, and the EWB field team.

Alongside the teams I keep building: a Romi robot, a hoverboard that floats on eddy currents, and now a drone that steers by tilting its propellers.

What keeps me coming back is the point where a team's idea starts working for real. I am chasing more of those.
